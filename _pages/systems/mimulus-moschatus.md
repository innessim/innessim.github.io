*Mimulus moschatus* doesn't get nearly the attention its cousin *M. guttatus* does — which is part of why I started working with it.

## Habitat & range

The musky monkeyflower is a perennial herb found throughout much of the western United States and Canada, broadly overlapping with *M. guttatus* but occupying a narrower overall distribution. It is less abundant at low elevations and across the full latitudinal range of the common yellow monkeyflower, but locally more common in the Sierra Nevada. Elsewhere, in the Cascades, Rockies, and coastal habitats, it is more widely scattered but less locally abundant. It is most at home in subalpine habitats, where water availability tracks winter snowpack accumulation and melt, with little to no precipitation through spring and summer.

## Natural history
Despite frequent overlap with *M. guttatus*, the two species differ in fine-scale habitat preference. Inland annual *M. guttatus* commonly occupies ephemeral streams, seeps, and rock walls that start very wet but dry quickly through the summer, giving many populations a short annual life cycle. *Mimulus moschatus*, by contrast, favours sites that start drier but retain moisture longer — extending its growing season relative to annual *M. guttatus* populations it frequently co-occurs with.


## Why I work with it

My dissertation used *M. moschatus* to ask how populations diverge and become reproductively isolated in the earliest stages of speciation — combining common-garden experiments, range-wide crossing designs, and population genomics. One of the more surprising findings was that geographic distance alone didn't predict how differentiated populations were. Some nearby populations were strongly diverged, while others separated by hundreds of kilometres showed little differentiation — a pattern that lines up better with historical demographic processes, like past glaciation, than with present-day geography or climate.
